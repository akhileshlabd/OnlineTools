Mathematical Foundations & Formula Architecture

Financial decisions rely on precise compound interest mathematics. For periodic recurring investments, the future value \(M\) is computed using the annuity formula \(M = P \times \frac{(1 + i)^n - 1}{i} \times (1 + i)\), where \(P\) represents regular periodic deposits, \(i\) is the periodic interest rate, and \(n\) is total compounding periods. For loan amortization (EMI), the monthly debt payment is determined by \(E = \frac{P \times r \times (1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1}\), precisely separating interest amortization from principal reduction. All math executes locally via double-precision 64-bit IEEE 754 floating-point operations in your browser, ensuring zero data leakage.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does compounding frequency affect total return?

More frequent compounding periods (such as monthly or daily versus annual) generate higher total yields over identical time horizons because interest earns interest sooner.

Are inflation or taxes accounted for in this projection?

The calculator reflects nominal returns. To estimate real purchasing power, subtract your local average annualized inflation rate from your nominal percentage return.

Is my personal financial information stored or tracked?

No. Free Qube calculators operate 100% locally on your machine. Your calculations are never transmitted to our servers or third parties.
