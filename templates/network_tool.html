Public IP Address & Geolocation: Technical Diagnostic Guide

Every device communicating over the global Internet requires a unique digital routing address. Your Public IP Address is assigned by your Internet Service Provider (ISP) and acts as the network identifier used by web servers, content delivery networks (CDNs), and peers to route responses back to your machine.

1. How It Works: BGP Routing & Autonomous System Numbers (ASN)

When you query your IP on Free Qube, our service identifies your socket connection metadata and resolves your network prefix against regional Internet registries (ARIN, RIPE, APNIC). Your ISP groups IP ranges into an Autonomous System Number (ASN), which routes traffic across Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) backbones.

  • IPv4 Routing: Standard 32-bit dotted-decimal notation (e.g., 198.51.100.1), mapped via subnet masking (CIDR).
  • IPv6 Dual-Stack: 128-bit hexadecimal notation (e.g., 2001:0db8:85a3::8a2e:0370:7334), ensuring end-to-end connectivity without Network Address Translation (NAT) bottlenecks.
  • Geolocation Triangulation: Cities and coordinates are mapped by cross-referencing ISP gateway hops without accessing device GPS hardware.

2. Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. Instant Diagnostic: Opening this tool immediately reveals your active public IPv4/IPv6 address.
  2. Inspect Network Metadata: Review your detected ISP, Autonomous System (ASN), country, and metro region.
  3. One-Click Copy: Click the "Copy IP Address" button to copy your IP for SSH, gaming, or whitelist configuration.
  4. Verify VPN/Proxy Status: If using a VPN or Tor, refresh this page to confirm your real ISP and location are successfully obscured.

4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between my public IP and private IP?

Your private IP (e.g., 192.168.1.X or 10.0.0.X) is assigned by your home Wi-Fi router for local device communication. Your public IP is the external address assigned to your modem by your ISP.

Can someone locate my physical home using my IP address?

No. IP geolocation resolves to the nearest ISP routing hub or cellular cell tower, typically indicating only the general city or region, not your street address or apartment number.

Why does my IP address change periodically?

Most residential internet connections use Dynamic IP allocation (DHCP). Your ISP automatically reassigns a new IP address when your router reboots or leases expire.

Browser-Based Bandwidth Benchmarking: Technical Guide

Network performance directly dictates high-definition video conferencing, low-latency gaming, and cloud file transfer speeds. Our Internet Speed Test measures real-world HTTP/HTTPS download throughput directly within your browser without requiring proprietary plugins.

1. How It Works: Multi-Chunk Throughput Stream Calculation

Rather than relying on inaccurate synthetic ping bursts, our speed test requests byte streams of increasing size (from 1MB to 50MB) across global edge nodes. The download speed is calculated using precise high-resolution performance timers (performance.now()):

\text{Speed (Mbps)} = \frac{\text{Bytes Received} \times 8}{\text{Elapsed Time (seconds)} \times 10^6}

The calculation accounts for TCP slow-start and network jitter to report true steady-state downstream bandwidth.

2. Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. Prepare Environment: Pause background downloads, VPN tunnels, and video streaming apps for optimal measurement.
  2. Start Benchmark: Click "Start Speed Test" to begin the download measurement.
  3. Observe Real-Time Gauge: Watch the live throughput gauge measure bandwidth stability.
  4. Analyze Final Metrics: Compare your measured Megabits per second (Mbps) with your ISP subscription tier.

4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?

Megabits per second (Mbps) measures network transfer speed, while Megabytes per second (MB/s) measures file size storage. Because there are 8 bits in a byte, a 100 Mbps connection downloads files at approximately 12.5 MB/s.

Why is Wi-Fi slower than my advertised internet plan?

Wi-Fi speeds degrade due to wall interference, distance from the router, and 2.4GHz radio congestion. For maximum speed matching your ISP plan, test via a wired Ethernet cable.

Does this test consume mobile cellular data?

Yes. Running a complete speed benchmark downloads between 10MB and 50MB of test payloads to accurately calibrate high-speed connections.

DNS Record Inspection & RFC 8484 Architecture: Technical Guide

The Domain Name System (DNS) is the critical decentralized directory that translates human-readable hostnames (such as freeqube.com) into network-routable IP addresses. Our DNS Record Inspector queries authoritative root nameservers using DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH).

1. How It Works: Cryptographic DNS-over-HTTPS (RFC 8484)

Traditional DNS queries are transmitted unencrypted over UDP port 53, leaving them vulnerable to ISP eavesdropping and Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) cache poisoning. Free Qube executes queries via RFC 8484 DNS-over-HTTPS JSON endpoints:

  • A Records: Resolves canonical 32-bit IPv4 host addresses.
  • AAAA Records: Resolves native 128-bit IPv6 host addresses.
  • CNAME Records: Inspects canonical alias redirects and CDN edge routing paths.
  • MX Records: Queries priority-weighted mail exchange servers for email delivery.
  • TXT Records: Validates domain ownership, SPF email policies, DKIM keys, and DMARC enforcement.

2. Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. Enter Domain Name: Type any valid apex domain or subdomain (e.g., example.com or mail.example.com).
  2. Select Record Type: Choose A, AAAA, CNAME, MX, or TXT from the record selector.
  3. Execute Query: Click "Lookup" to dispatch an encrypted DoH query.
  4. Inspect TTL & Data: Review the Time-To-Live (TTL) cache expiration and raw record payloads in the response table.

4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is DNS Propagation and why does it take up to 48 hours?

When you update DNS records at your registrar, worldwide caching resolvers store old records until their TTL (Time-To-Live) expires. Full worldwide propagation can take from a few minutes to 48 hours.

What does an MX record priority number mean?

MX priority numbers determine routing order (e.g., 1, 5, 10). Sending mail servers always attempt delivery to the lowest priority number first, using higher numbers as fallbacks.

Can I check SPF and DKIM records with this tool?

Yes! Query TXT records to view your domain's SPF (v=spf1 ...) configuration and verify email deliverability settings.

What is the difference between screen resolution and browser viewport?

Screen resolution is the total physical pixel dimensions of your monitor or phone display. Viewport size is the actual inner space available inside your browser window excluding toolbars and scrollbars.

Does this tool track my identity across other websites?

No. Free Qube does not generate persistent browser fingerprints, track behavioral history, or store device profiles.

Why does my user agent string show older operating systems?

Modern browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox) freeze user-agent strings to prevent cross-site user tracking, reporting generalized platform identifiers for privacy.
